# UC-021 — Learn without relying on fluent reading and spelling

<!-- id: UC-021 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** a learner with dyslexia, or anyone for whom typing a word correctly is
a separate difficulty from knowing it.
**Wants to:** be tested on whether they know the word, not on whether they can
spell it.
**So that:** their vocabulary is measured as vocabulary.

Derived from [`../studie/14-barrierefreiheit.md`](../studie/14-barrierefreiheit.md).

## Today

Typing is the only way to answer a production card in most apps, so spelling
silently becomes part of every vocabulary measurement. A learner who knows a
word and mistypes it is recorded as not knowing it, their scheduler pushes it
back at them, and the app concludes they are struggling with vocabulary.

## Success looks like

- Every card can be answered by **speaking or selecting** as well as typing, and
  all three count equally toward whether the word is known.
- A spelling mistake on a correct answer is reported as a spelling mistake, not
  as a failed recall, and does not by itself shorten the interval.
- Audio is available for every text, not only for material labelled as listening
  practice — so reading-while-listening is always possible.
- Typography is adjustable: typeface, size, line spacing, line length, background
  tone. No claim is attached to any particular font.
- Sound-to-spelling correspondences are available as their own explicit,
  structured content, because explicit instruction is where the evidence is
  strongest here.
- Nothing in the product imposes a time limit on answering.

## Out of scope

Diagnosing dyslexia, native-language literacy training, and treating "learning
style" preferences as an equivalent need — a required alternative route and a
preferred modality are different things
([`../studie/16-weitere-befunde.md`](../studie/16-weitere-befunde.md) W1).
