# UC-036 — Know how much to trust this language's material

<!-- id: UC-036 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** anyone learning a language whose course was bootstrapped rather than
built.
**Wants to:** know which parts are solid and which are machine-made guesses.
**So that:** they can decide what to rely on — and are not quietly told a
precise level that rests on nothing.

Derived from
[`../study/18-language-kit.md`](../study/18-language-kit.md) and
[`../study/10-antipatterns.md`](../study/10-antipatterns.md) A5.

## Today

Generated course content is now normal across the industry, shipped with quality
trade-offs that are stated in press releases and nowhere in the product. The
learner is structurally unable to detect an error in the language they are
learning, so they cannot compensate for what they are not told.

## Success looks like

- Each language carries a quality tier, **derived** from what its profile
  actually contains — never a field someone sets by hand.
- The tier is visible where it matters and says what it means in concrete terms,
  not as a badge: which parts are checked, which are generated, what the level
  figure rests on.
- At the lowest tier — no reliable lemmatiser — the app still works for cards and
  reading, but reports **no level value at all**. The skill reads "not measured"
  rather than showing a number derived from a count that has no defined unit.
- Generated content is marked as generated wherever it appears, and reporting it
  is one action (UC-023).
- Improving a language's data visibly raises its tier, and the change is dated —
  a learner's history must not appear to jump because the calibration improved.

## Out of scope

Hiding lower-tier languages, and blocking a learner from a language because its
data is incomplete. The answer to thin data is to say so, not to refuse.
