# UC-044 — Say the same thing three times and hear myself get better

<!-- id: UC-044 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** a learner who freezes when speaking, or speaks in fragments.
**Wants to:** practise talking without a partner, a tutor or an appointment.
**So that:** fluency develops from repetition rather than waiting for the
courage to talk to someone.

Derived from
[`../study/STUDY-018-speaking-and-sentences.md`](../study/STUDY-018-speaking-and-sentences.md) S1.

## Today

Speaking practice needs a person or an AI. Both are expensive, one is
intimidating, and the AI has to be told to correct anything. Meanwhile the
best-evidenced fluency exercise needs neither: telling the same story three
times, in four minutes, then three, then two, reliably raises speech rate,
lowers hesitation, reduces errors in the repeated parts — and produces *more*
complex grammar in the last round than the first.

## Success looks like

- The learner picks or is given a topic at their level and gets **planning
  time** before the first round.
- Three rounds, with a visible countdown: four minutes, three, two.
- Each round is recorded. Between rounds there is no feedback and no correction
  — the mechanism is repetition freeing attention, not being told what was wrong.
- Afterwards the three recordings can be played back-to-back. Hearing the
  difference is the point; no score is shown.
- Speech rate and pause count are computed and shown as a change across the
  three rounds, not as a grade.
- Recordings stay on the device unless the learner chooses otherwise, and where
  they are processed is stated (`CONSTITUTION` §2).
- The whole exercise works offline and with no other person involved.
- Words the learner reached for and did not find can be captured afterwards, as
  in UC-043.

## Out of scope

Pronunciation scoring (UC-014 covers what is reliable), conversation, grammar
correction during the rounds, and comparing recordings against a native speaker.
