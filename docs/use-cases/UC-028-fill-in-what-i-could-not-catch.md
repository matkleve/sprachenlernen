# UC-028 — Fill in the words I could not catch

<!-- id: UC-028 -->
<!-- specs: SPEC-service-material-unit, SPEC-feature-exercise-runner, SPEC-feature-listening-defer -->

**Who:** a learner who can read a transcript fine and still misses half of it by
ear.
**Wants to:** practise catching words in real speech, and find out which ones
they systematically miss.
**So that:** their vocabulary stops being something that only exists on paper.

Derived from [`../study/17-own-content.md`](../study/17-own-content.md)
and [`../study/04-flashcards-srs.md`](../study/04-flashcards-srs.md).

## Today

Listening practice is comprehension questions, which can be answered from
context without catching a single word precisely. The alternative — full
dictation — is slow and mostly retypes words the learner already hears fine.
Neither says *which* words are the problem.

## Success looks like

- The learner gets a **half-filled sentence** (or window of sentences) and fills
  gaps while listening — or **types only** when listening defer is active (UC-077).
- Input: **type** (default) or **speak** when sound is available.
- Gaps are **chosen, never random**. They target words the learner knows in
  writing but not by ear, words containing an unsolved sound contrast, and words
  learned recently.
- Audio can be replayed per sentence without leaving the exercise.
- Every missed gap becomes an audio-recall card, and repeated misses of the same
  contrast route into perception training (UC-014).
- The exercise reports what kind of miss it was — the word was unknown, or known
  but not recognised in speech. Those are different problems with different
  fixes.
- The passage is chosen at a coverage level where the gaps are the difficulty,
  not the surrounding text.

## Out of scope

Grading spelling as part of the score (UC-021 applies), full dictation of long
passages, and correcting a machine transcript — which sounds similar and has no
evidence behind it.
