# UC-054 — Find out whether this is going anywhere at all

<!-- id: UC-054 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** someone who has practised diligently for months and cannot tell whether
it amounts to anything.
**Wants to:** see the honest rate — what the time spent buys, against what the
target actually costs.
**So that:** they can decide to spend more time, change the target, or stop,
instead of quietly suspecting they are wasting their evenings.

Derived from
[`../study/25-why-it-does-not-feel-productive.md`](../study/25-why-it-does-not-feel-productive.md)

## Today

Every app shows a numerator — days, XP, lessons, words seen — and no denominator.
Fifteen minutes a day is 91 hours a year; B1 is a 350–400 hour band. Nobody puts
those two numbers on one screen, because a denominator is the one display that
cannot be optimised for daily return. The learner is left comparing a rising
counter against an unstated expectation, which is exactly the arrangement in which
diligence feels like failure.

## Success looks like

- Hours practised are shown **against the band for the stated target level**, not
  alone, and not as a percentage that hides the width of the band.
- The band is labelled as what it is: an institutional, English-derived estimate
  used as an order of magnitude. A learner who asks where the number comes from
  gets an answer, not a citation-free bar.
- The arithmetic is reversible on screen: at the current rate, the target lands
  roughly *here*. Changing the daily minutes changes that date visibly.
- The honest conclusion is allowed to be discouraging. If the rate says four
  years, it says four years, and the next sentence is about options — a smaller
  target, more time, or a different definition of done — never encouragement.
- Practice time is counted from stage 1 onward, because it cannot be
  reconstructed later.
- No streak, no comparison with other learners, and the figure is never the
  headline of the home screen.

## Out of scope

Predicting an individual's learning speed, promising a date, and adjusting the
band to flatter the learner's history.
