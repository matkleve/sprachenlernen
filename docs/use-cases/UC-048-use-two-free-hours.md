# UC-048 — Use two free hours well

<!-- id: UC-048 -->
<!-- specs: SPEC-page-method-menu, SPEC-service-time-scale -->

**Who:** a learner with an unexpectedly free afternoon.
**Wants to:** spend it on the language and get something out of it.
**So that:** the rare long window is worth more than twenty short sessions.

Derived from
[`../study/21-method-catalogue-and-context.md`](../study/21-method-catalogue-and-context.md)
and [`../study/02-evidence.md`](../study/02-evidence.md) E2.

## Today

Every app is built for five minutes. Given two hours, they offer the five-minute
thing twenty-four times — which is worse than useless, because massed repetition
of the same cards is exactly what spacing research says not to do. The learner
with time gets no more from the product than the learner without.

## Success looks like

- A long window is a **different offer**, not more of the short one: the methods
  that only work with time — a full dictation, a piece of writing, a long read,
  a 4/3/2 round with real preparation, an hour with one difficult text. On the
  method menu, drag time to **Endless** (or up to **one day**) to surface them.
- It is composed as a **sequence with a shape**: something demanding while fresh,
  something receptive after, something producing at the end.
- Card review is a small part of it, not the bulk. Extra cards today do not
  substitute for cards tomorrow, and the app says so rather than letting the
  learner grind.
- The plan is visible before starting and can be reshaped.
- Breaks are built in and stated.
- Stopping halfway is a complete outcome, not an abandoned session.

## Out of scope

Marathon or challenge framing, streak-style rewards for long sessions, and
letting a long session pull tomorrow's reviews forward beyond what the scheduler
allows.
