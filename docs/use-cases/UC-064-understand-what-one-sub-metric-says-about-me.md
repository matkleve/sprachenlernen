# UC-064 — Understand what one sub-metric says about me

<!-- id: UC-064 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** a learner who has opened a level, followed it down to a single signal,
and is now looking at something like "plural forms: 3 of 10".
**Wants to:** know what that number is counting, how sure it is, and what it does
**not** mean.
**So that:** a diagnostic figure does not become a score to farm.

Derived from [`../study/STUDY-003-level-model.md`](../study/STUDY-003-level-model.md) (layer 1
is the only measured layer) and
[`../study/STUDY-023-why-it-does-not-feel-productive.md`](../study/STUDY-023-why-it-does-not-feel-productive.md)
(a metric shown prominently gets optimised, and cheap metrics get optimised
first). The immediate parent is UC-062.

## Today

Apps either show no sub-metrics at all, or show a wall of them with no
derivation and no denominator — mastery percentages whose base nobody states. A
figure with a hidden denominator cannot be checked, so it is either believed or
ignored, and both are wrong.

## Success looks like

- The figure names its **denominator** in words. "3 of 10" is meaningless until
  the ten are identifiable — ten cell groups, ten of the learner's own words,
  ten tested items.
- It is **frequency-weighted, never counted**. A completion fraction pulls effort
  toward what is left rather than what pays, and paradigm cells are wildly
  unequal — the first-person singular present earns its practice thousands of
  times more often than the second-person plural subjunctive
  ([26](../study/STUDY-024-readiness-and-difficulty.md) R8). The shape of the widget
  teaches a goal whatever the copy says.
- It states the **task type it was measured on**, and no claim crosses from
  receptive evidence to productive competence
  ([26](../study/STUDY-024-readiness-and-difficulty.md) R9).
- It names what counts as having one: stability above some threshold, a number of
  successful retrievals, a share correct. Whatever it is, it is one documented,
  dated definition, and the same definition everywhere it appears.
- It shows its uncertainty. Early on, a signal computed from four observations
  reads as such rather than as a precise fraction.
- It says what it does not cover. A plural signal computed only from recognition
  items does not license a claim about production, and says so.
- **It is not a target.** No goal may be set on it, nothing celebrates it moving,
  and the app never proposes raising it as an end in itself. It exists to explain
  a level, not to be pursued.
- Its status is one of the four, at this granularity: measured · uncertain · not
  measured · not in profile. "Not measured" is the honest reading of most signals
  for most learners for a long time, and it never renders as zero.
- When the definition changes, the change is dated and the old line stays visible
  — honesty rule 4. A sub-metric silently redefined is worse than one never
  shown, because the learner has already built a belief on it.

## Out of scope

Displaying every layer-1 signal by default; sub-metrics on the home surface; and
exporting a sub-metric as a shareable figure — the moment it leaves the app as a
number it stops carrying its denominator.

## Undecided

- **⚠ SPEC GAP: what "3 of 10" measures, and whether it exists at all.** The
  owner's illustration was explicitly a placeholder — *"submetrics or something,
  I don't know"*. The candidate readings (cell groups held · own words inflected
  correctly · items answered on a tested set) differ in what they license, and
  the whole family of readiness and guidance decisions in UC-057, UC-060 and
  UC-061 inherits the choice. It is the single unresolved quantity underneath all
  of them.
- **⚠ SPEC GAP: whether a signal has a natural denominator at all.** Vocabulary
  size is an estimate over an open set with no denominator; form mastery over a
  closed paradigm has one. Presenting both as fractions would invent a bound for
  the first.
