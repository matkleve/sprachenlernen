# UC-038 — Know why this word is worth my time

<!-- id: UC-038 -->
<!-- specs: SPEC-feature-word-detail, SPEC-feature-content-traceability -->

**Who:** a learner looking at a card and wondering whether it earns its place.
**Wants to:** see what this word gets them.
**So that:** they trust the deck instead of second-guessing it, and stop
collecting words that will never appear again.

Derived from
[`../study/STUDY-017-milestones-and-map.md`](../study/STUDY-017-milestones-and-map.md)
and [`../study/STUDY-004-flashcards-srs.md`](../study/STUDY-004-flashcards-srs.md).

## Today

Self-built decks fill with words met once, in contexts that never recur, while
common words are missed because they were never noticed. Supplied decks give no
reason for their order. Either way the learner has no basis for judging whether
a card deserves the next five years of reviews.

## Success looks like

- Any word can be opened to show its frequency rank, its block, and — the part
  that matters — **where it appears in the learner's own saved content**.
- A word that occurs in several things the learner wants to understand is
  visibly worth more than one that occurs nowhere, and the deck order reflects
  that.
- A rare word the learner added deliberately is kept without argument. The
  information is offered, not enforced.
- A word can be dropped from the deck in one action, and dropping it does not
  delete its history.
- Where a word is part of a fixed expression, that is shown — learning the
  expression is usually the better buy.

## Out of scope

Automatically removing rare words, refusing to add a word, and ranking words by
anything other than frequency and the learner's own content.
