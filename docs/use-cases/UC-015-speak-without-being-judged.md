# UC-015 — Say something in the language without an audience

<!-- id: UC-015 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** a learner who can read and understand far more than they can say.
**Wants to:** hold a conversation, fail in it, and be corrected.
**So that:** they find out what they cannot yet express — which is the thing
that makes them notice it in everything they read and hear afterwards.

Derived from [`../study/STUDY-006-production.md`](../study/STUDY-006-production.md) and
[`../study/STUDY-014-further-findings.md`](../study/STUDY-014-further-findings.md) W4.

## Today

Speaking practice means repeating a given sentence. Free production happens only
with another person, which costs money, scheduling, and — for most learners —
more nerve than they have. Anxiety is not a side issue here: the pressure of
being watched is one of the strongest inhibitors of speaking there is.

## Success looks like

- A conversation can be started at any moment, on a topic at the learner's level,
  with no other person involved and no appointment.
- A visible **correction setting** with three positions — let me talk / gentle /
  strict — changeable mid-conversation. It is a control the learner can see, not
  a hidden prompt.
- On "gentle", corrections are marked as corrections. A reformulation the learner
  does not notice is not feedback.
- Errors are collected and shown **after** the conversation, grouped by category,
  with the recurring ones turned into cards.
- The debrief names what went **well**, with a comparison to previous sessions,
  before it names errors.
- The first conversation includes a short, skippable explanation of how to get
  useful correction out of it.
- Nothing in the debrief is phrased as a shortfall against a target.

## Out of scope

Voice conversation (later), pronunciation assessment (UC-014 covers the part
that is reliable), matching learners with real people, and any claim that this
replaces speaking with humans.
