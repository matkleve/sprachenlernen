# UC-047 — Choose something demanding, on purpose

<!-- id: UC-047 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** a learner with real time and real motivation today.
**Wants to:** do the hard thing — a full dictation, a dictogloss, a
back-translation, an hour with one difficult text.
**So that:** the ceiling on their practice is their own capacity, not what an
app considered palatable.

Derived from
[`../study/21-method-catalogue-and-context.md`](../study/21-method-catalogue-and-context.md),
"Warum Apps die harten Methoden meiden".

## Today

Demanding methods are slow, produce visible failure, and cannot be gamified — so
products measured on engagement cannot offer them, whatever their didactic
merit. The learner who wants to work hard has to leave the app to do it, and
then nothing they did counts.

## Success looks like

- Demanding methods are present, first-class, and marked as demanding rather
  than hidden behind an "advanced" wall.
- Their info page says plainly what they cost and why it is worth it.
- Failure inside them is normal and framed as such. A dictation with eleven
  mistakes is a good dictation; the mistakes are the yield.
- Their results feed back — errors become cards, and the work counts toward the
  same signals as anything else.
- Nothing about choosing a hard method is rewarded with points or celebration.
  The reward is that it worked, shown later on the map (UC-033).
- The app never quietly substitutes an easier variant because the learner
  struggled.

## Out of scope

Difficulty settings as a global switch, and any implication that a learner who
picks lighter methods is doing it wrong.
