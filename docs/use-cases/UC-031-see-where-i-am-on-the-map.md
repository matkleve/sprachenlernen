# UC-031 — See where I am on the map of the language

<!-- id: UC-031 -->
<!-- specs: SPEC-feature-words-home, SPEC-service-frequency-blocks -->

**Who:** a learner months in, who knows they are progressing but not what that
means.
**Wants to:** see where their vocabulary sits in the language as a whole, and
how the pieces of their practice connect.
**So that:** learning stops feeling like an endless list and starts having a
shape.

Derived from
[`../study/19-milestones-and-map.md`](../study/19-milestones-and-map.md).

## Today

Every product gives a **compass**: direction, progress, keep going. None gives a
**map**. The learner cannot see where they stand in the language, what is
reachable from here, or how a word learned this morning connects to the podcast
they could not follow last month. So they lose the thread and, with it, the
sense that any of it adds up.

## Success looks like

- One surface shows the learner's vocabulary against the frequency distribution
  of the language: the solid zone, the holes in it, and the edge being worked on.
- Frequent words the learner does *not* yet know are visibly the holes — they are
  the highest-value cards in the deck and should look like it.
- Any word can be traced in both directions: where it appears in the learner's
  own content, what rank and block it belongs to, and why it was scheduled.
- The surface names the loop rather than assuming it: a word learned raises
  coverage, coverage moves content into reach, content in reach produces more
  words. Each display points at the next.
- Everything shown has a textual equivalent — a scatter plot must not be the only
  way to read the core information (UC-021, and `CONSTITUTION` §3).

## v1 scope (decided 2026-08-12)

The first map is **pool-local**: frequency bands over the starter deck on
`/words`, not language-wide coverage. Bands show how many words the learner holds
stably in each chunk of the 2,000-word pool. Language-wide holes, content
traceability, and coverage payoff lines ship with the coverage calculator
(stage 3).

## Out of scope

Comparison with other learners, gamified map metaphors with regions to
"conquer", and any figure that cannot be derived from review data.
