# UC-045 — Practise in the situation I am actually in

<!-- id: UC-045 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** anyone whose day contains very different fifteen-minute windows.
**Wants to:** be offered only what is possible right now — paper exercises at
the desk, hands-free audio while cooking, quiet tapping on a crowded tram.
**So that:** the offer is usable rather than aspirational, and the good windows
of the day stop being wasted.

Derived from
[`../study/21-method-catalogue-and-context.md`](../study/21-method-catalogue-and-context.md).

## Today

Apps assume one situation: a person alone, looking at a phone, with a free hand
and no reason to be quiet. Everything is built for that, so the forty-five
minutes spent cooking — eyes and hands gone, voice and time free — offer
nothing, while a dictation is proposed on a bus.

## Success looks like

- One tap sets the current situation from named presets the learner recognises —
  at the desk, walking, on transit, at the computer, in bed, waiting, cooking.
- Presets are editable, and the learner can create their own.
- The situation is described by what is **available**: eyes, hands, voice,
  writing surface, sound, attention, time, company.
- Methods that cannot be performed here are **not offered at all**, not offered
  and then greyed out.
- Situation filters **before** anything else — before the floor, before measured
  effect, before preference. A perfect method that cannot be done right now has
  an effect of zero.
- Situations where the app currently has nothing good say so, and name the
  nearest thing, instead of padding the menu.
- The situation is **stated by the learner**, never inferred. No location, no
  motion sensing, no time-of-day profiling.

## Out of scope

Detecting context automatically, learning routines from behaviour, and any
permission request in service of guessing where someone is.
