# UC-052 — Change something about my life, not just my day

<!-- id: UC-052 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** someone who already practises regularly and wants more out of the same
hours.
**Wants to:** adopt one or two standing rules — write to a friend only in the
target language, switch the phone's language, think the shopping list through in
it.
**So that:** the language runs in the background of ordinary life instead of only
inside a session.

Derived from
[`../study/24-speaking-as-the-goal.md`](../study/24-speaking-as-the-goal.md) S4.

## Today

These are the cheapest high-leverage things a learner can do, and they live in
blog posts. Nobody adopts them because nobody suggests them at a moment when they
would land. Apps have no place to put them: everything in an app is a session
with a duration and a completion, and a commitment has neither.

## Success looks like

- Commitments are a **second kind of catalogue entry**, distinct from methods:
  standing rather than timed, active/inactive rather than done/not done.
- They are found under a heading about getting more out of the learning, not on
  the daily menu — the menu is for sessions
  ([UC-010](UC-010-choose-how-to-practise-today.md)).
- Each one says what it is supposed to do and how confident we are, exactly like
  a method's info page ([UC-042](UC-042-know-why-a-method-works.md)) — including
  what it does **not** do.
- **At most two or three active at once.** Adopting a fourth means dropping one,
  and the app says so.
- **No completion tracking and no streak.** A commitment that gets ticked daily
  has become a method with extra steps.
- After a few weeks, one quiet review — *"still writing to Marco in Italian?"* —
  where keeping, changing and dropping are equally normal answers.
- Dropping one is never framed as a lapse.

## Out of scope

Verifying that a commitment is being kept, counting it toward the level model,
reminders, and social features around it.
