# UC-008 — Listen to a story while walking, cooking or commuting

<!-- id: UC-008 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** anyone with dead time and no free hands or eyes.
**Wants to:** listen to something in the target language and still be able to
say "again", "slower" or "what does that mean" without looking at a screen.
**So that:** the largest block of available learning time in a normal week stops
being unusable.

Derived from [`../study/05-input-reading-listening.md`](../study/05-input-reading-listening.md).

## Today

Podcasts and audiobooks in the target language are either too hard or too
boring, and there is no way to react to a sentence you did not catch. Rewinding
by a fixed number of seconds is a poor substitute for "repeat that sentence".
Learners give up and listen in their own language instead.

## Success looks like

- Audio is selected by the same coverage calculation as text (UC-007).
- The transcript is available in three switchable states: audio only,
  audio + target-language transcript, audio + translation.
- Which state was used is recorded, because listening with a translation
  visible does not prove the same thing as listening without.
- Spoken commands — repeat, slower, faster, translate, what does *X* mean, save
  that, continue — respond in **under a second**, and are recognised in the
  user's own language.
- The same commands exist as physical controls: on-screen, lock-screen and
  headphone buttons. Nothing is voice-only.
- The whole session is operable without ever looking at the screen.
- Sentences the user repeated several times are offered afterwards as cards.

## Out of scope

Open-ended conversation with the app, importing personal audiobooks, and
pronunciation assessment of the user's own speech.
