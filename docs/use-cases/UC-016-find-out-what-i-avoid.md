# UC-016 — Find out what I have been avoiding

<!-- id: UC-016 -->
<!-- specs:  -->

**Who:** an intermediate learner who has become fluent-sounding and stopped
improving.
**Wants to:** see what they systematically go around instead of through.
**So that:** they stop mistaking a shrinking error rate for growing competence.

Derived from [`../studie/06-produktion.md`](../studie/06-produktion.md),
"Umschifft".

## Today

Nothing measures this, anywhere. Learners get fluent partly by avoiding
everything they cannot do — a tense they are unsure of, a word they half-know,
a construction they never mastered. Error counts fall, which every app reads as
progress, while the actual range stops growing. A human teacher notices; no
software does.

## Success looks like

- After a conversation or a piece of writing, the learner sees not only errors
  but **evasions**: a vaguer word used where a precise one existed, a structure
  consistently routed around, a tense never attempted.
- Each evasion comes with the alternatives that would have fit, offered as cards.
- Back-translation exercises are used for this deliberately: comparing the
  learner's version against a reference makes evasions visible in a checkable
  form, which conversation alone does not.
- The framing is opportunity, not deficiency — "here is what you could have
  said", never "here is what you failed to say" .
- Evasion patterns are tracked over time, so a construction that was being
  avoided and now is not shows up as progress.

## Out of scope

Anything below B1 — a beginner avoiding structures they have never met is not
avoidance, it is simply not knowing them yet, and flagging it would be
discouraging and wrong.
