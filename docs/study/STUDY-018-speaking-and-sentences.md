## Thesis

Core speaking practice can work without an AI tutor at the centre.

## Evidence

Findings marked `[A]`–`[D]` appear inline in the sections below.

## Product consequences

[06](STUDY-006-production.md) says *why* production is necessary. This chapter says
*how* — which speaking exercises demonstrably work, and what follows from each
for the product.

The finding that surprised me most, up front: **the most effective speaking
exercise needs no LLM, no speech recognition and no conversation partner. It
needs a stopwatch.**

---

## How sentences come about

Not assembled word by word. Fluent speakers retrieve **prefabricated building
blocks** — collocations, fixed expressions, sentence frames. These are
demonstrably processed faster than freely assembled language, lower cognitive
load and are therefore the actual source of speaking rate
([16](STUDY-014-further-findings.md), W2).

For sentence exercises something concrete follows: an exercise that has the
learner construct every sentence from scratch trains construction. An exercise
that drills **recurring building blocks** trains fluency. Both are needed, they
are not the same thing, and the second is missing from almost every app.

---

## S1 · Task repetition: the 4/3/2 technique **[B] — best effort-to-effect ratio**

You tell the same story three times: once in **four** minutes, then **three**,
then **two**.

What measurably happens:

- **Speech rate rises** (words per minute).
- **Hesitation falls** (pauses per 100 words).
- **Grammatical errors in the repeated parts decrease.**
- And the most interesting finding: in the last round learners use **more
  complex** constructions than in the first — despite having less time.

The mechanism explains why: after the first round the **content** is settled. The
attention previously spent on inventing is freed for **form**. The shortened time
also enforces pace without anyone saying "faster!".

A quasi-experimental study found significant improvements in speaking fluency
**and speaking confidence**, and learners perceived the repetition as
anxiety-reducing — consistent with [16](STUDY-014-further-findings.md), W4.

> **For us:** this is a complete method with a timer and a record button. No LLM,
> no ASR, no second person, runs offline, buildable in an hour. It belongs among
> the first speaking functions, not the last.
>
> **Adaptation:** without a listener, the social pressure the original generates
> with three different audiences is missing. Substitute: play your own three
> recordings back to back. The difference is clearly audible, and that is the
> competence moment from [08](STUDY-008-motivation.md), M5 in its purest form.

## S2 · Planning time before speaking **[B]**

Time to prepare improves the complexity, accuracy and fluency of the subsequent
utterance. Costs nothing but a pause in the flow.

> **For us:** every speaking task has a visible preparation phase. Starting
> immediately is a mode, not the default.

---

## S3 · Task-based language teaching **[B, with a caveat]**

Learning to speak through real tasks — find something out, reach agreement,
organise something — rather than practice sentences. A meta-analysis reports a
medium-to-large effect on oral production (d ≈ 0.78), with gains in fluency,
accuracy and willingness to communicate.

The caveat belongs with it: a 2023 paper asks openly in its title whether the
*relative* effectiveness of TBLT programmes can be estimated meta-analytically at
all. The direction is solid; the number less so.

> **For us:** speaking tasks have an **outcome**, not just a topic. "Talk about
> holidays" is not a task. "Plan a weekend in Naples with me; we have to agree on
> three things" is one.

---

## S4 · Shadowing **[B for flow, unclear for individual sounds]**

Speaking along with the audio at a minimal delay. A systematic review finds:
**all eight studies on fluency report positive results**, as do those on
comprehensibility and prosody. For **individual sounds** the findings are
explicitly inconclusive. Learners find it interesting and enjoyable — rare among
effective methods.

> **For us:** shadowing and HVPT ([13](STUDY-011-pronunciation-perception.md)) are
> **complementary, not interchangeable**. HVPT trains perception of individual
> sounds, where shadowing achieves nothing clear; shadowing trains rhythm, melody
> and flow, where HVPT achieves nothing. Building only one builds half.
>
> And it is cheap: we have audio with a synchronised transcript anyway
> ([05](STUDY-005-input-reading-listening.md)).

---

## S5 · Drama and role play **[B/C]**

Your idea, and it has research behind it.

Process drama lowers foreign language anxiety: through the role, the authentic
context and the dramatic tension, more spontaneous communication emerges. A study
with Brazilian EFL learners found significant gains in **oral fluency** in the
drama group where the control group remained unchanged, and a somewhat stronger
reduction in anxiety.

The honest caveat from the same literature: anxiety is intensively researched,
but **interventions** against it barely are — it is unclear *how strongly* drama
actually affects it.

Why it works nonetheless is well arguable: in a role, it is not you speaking. The
mistake belongs to the character. That is precisely the exit from the ought-to
self in [16](STUDY-014-further-findings.md), W4 — and the reason people who dare not
speak suddenly talk in role play.

> **For us:** an app cannot host writing and performing a play — and should not.
> It can **propose, prepare and debrief** it. See [12](STUDY-010-method-cards.md),
> "Methods beyond the app".

## What this chapter says about the product

The four cheapest speaking functions are also the best evidenced, and **none of
them needs AI**:

| Method | Needs | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4/3/2 | stopwatch, microphone | **[B]**, very concrete |
| Planning time | a pause in the flow | **[B]** |
| Shadowing | audio + transcript (we have it) | **[B]** for flow |
| Self-comparison of recordings | two play buttons | **[C]**, but free |

That shifts [11](../backlog/BL-011-roadmap-open-questions.md): speaking had to wait for stage 5
because I had coupled it to the LLM conversation partner. That was wrong. **The
evidenced core of speaking practice is independent of AI and can come with stage
4** — the conversation partner is an extension, not a prerequisite.
