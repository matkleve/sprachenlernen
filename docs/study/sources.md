# Sources

Honestly marked by how solid each citation is. That is unusual, and it is why
this study can be trusted more than a bibliography that says nothing about how
far its entries were checked.

| Level | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **⬤ checked** | The source was opened while writing this study; title, location and key claim come from it |
| **◐ secondary** | The key claim comes from a summary or citation inside a checked source, not from the original |
| **○ from memory** | Standard literature, cited from memory and **not** checked in this session. Look it up before using it anywhere published |

A **○** does not mean unreliable — these are usually the best-known works in the
field. It means the year, the exact effect size or the precise wording may be
off.

## What is missing

For honesty, this study's remaining gaps:

- **No primary research on sub-level vocabulary anchors.** The table in
  [03](03-level-model.md) is **[C]** and must be recalibrated empirically per
  language.
- **No cost or market research.** This study says nothing about whether the
  product is viable.
- ~~No research on accessibility~~ → done in [14](14-accessibility.md).
  **Partly** closed: dyslexia is evidenced; hearing and vision impairment in a
  foreign-language context are not — those recommendations are **[D]**.
- **No research on children and adolescents.** Everything here assumes adult,
  self-directed learners. One sign that this does not transfer automatically is in
  [16](16-further-findings.md) W3: the sleep method worked in adults and **not**
  in adolescents.
- **No research on age effects or the critical period.** Without consequence for
  the product decisions here, but not for managing user expectations.
- **No cost or market research**, and [15](15-landscape.md) does not substitute
  for it — that chapter is about feature scope, not viability.
