# 07 · Offline and paper

Your observation: *"not everything has to be on the phone — maybe also exercises
where you take dictation, write on a sheet of paper, or work through comparative
forms."*

This is the part no learning app has, and the reason is a business model rather
than didactics: an app that tells the user to put the phone down lowers its own
usage time. If we optimise competence instead of return ([01](01-duolingo.md),
D1), that contradiction disappears — and the area becomes a genuine
differentiator.

---

## The base pattern: prepare → do offline → feed back

A paper exercise is only more than a PDF for the app if the result comes back.
The loop has to close, or it is a craft project.

```
   1  App assembles it          from your card holdings and your errors
   2  You do it offline         paper, pen, headphones, no screen
   3  Reconciliation            photograph it  or  self-correct on screen
   4  Result feeds back         errors → cards · data → level ([03](03-level-model.md))
```

Step 3 is the critical one. **[D]** Recommendation: start with
**self-correction** — show the answer, the user ticks what was wrong. That is
immediately buildable, works for every exercise type and every handwriting.
Photo recognition is a later convenience, not a foundation; it fails on
handwriting, lighting and accents, and would hang the whole idea on a technology
it does not need.

A side finding in favour of self-correction: comparing your own answer against
the key is itself a processing act — probably more valuable than an automatic
tick.

> **Later correction:** for **free writing** the reasoning is different — see
> [23](23-how-an-exercise-runs.md). There is no key to compare against, so a
> photograph plus correction is the point rather than a shortcut around
> self-marking.

## Ü1 · Dictation **[B]**

The strongest offline exercise, because it couples listening and writing and
**forces** attention onto form. You cannot write a dictation approximately
right.

Yu, Boers & Tremblay (2025) found, for acquiring multiword items, that dictation
and dictogloss beat comprehension questions on the immediate test, with the lead
shrinking on the delayed test. The reading ([02](02-evidence.md), E9):
**dictation is an encoding technique, not a retention technique.** It supplies
attention; retention is supplied by the SRS. Exactly that combination is our
advantage — a dictation notebook alone does not have it.

**Procedure:** 6–10 sentences, read three times (once normally, once slowly with
pauses, once normally to check), controllable with the voice commands from
[05](05-input-reading-listening.md). Then the answer key on screen; tap errors →
cards.

**The trick:** the sentences are generated from your own card holdings — 90 %
known words, plus deliberately the words whose sound form is not yet secure.
Dictation thereby becomes a diagnosis: it finds exactly the words you *read* but
do not *hear* ([04](04-flashcards-srs.md), audio recall).

### Dictogloss as the harder version (from B1)

Do not transcribe; listen, take notes, then **reconstruct** the text from memory.
Combines listening, note-taking, grammar application and self-comparison against
the original. For advanced learners considerably more productive than plain
dictation, because it demands understanding rather than transcription.

## Ü2 · Handwritten vocabulary work **[B, with a caveat]**

Van der Meer & Van Weel (2024) show wider brain connectivity for handwriting
than typing. The caveat belongs with it and is in [02](02-evidence.md), E8: what
was measured was activity, not learning outcome.

So: an **offer, not a required station**, and particularly worthwhile for

- **non-Latin scripts** (Cyrillic, Greek, kana, hanzi) — here the evidence is
  clearest and the motor trace is part of the content;
- **stubborn cards** ("leeches", [04](04-flashcards-srs.md)) — a different
  modality breaks the entrenched wrong association;
- **orthography** in languages with irregular spelling.

Format: a printable sheet with the week's 20 shakiest cards, target word
covered, with a context sentence.

## Ü3 · Filling in paradigm tables yourself

Your idea about comparative forms. Conjugation, declension, comparison:
classical, unfashionable, and for what it is meant to achieve very efficient —
namely **automatisation**. Forms do not need to be understood but retrieved
quickly, and that is a drill problem, not a comprehension problem.

The one important design rule follows from [02](02-evidence.md), E6: **not in
blocks.** A table of only regular -ar verbs produces good training performance
and poor retention.

**The two halves of "mixed" are not equally supported, and were graded together
until 2026-08-11.** *Several tenses side by side* is the manipulation the
interleaving studies on Romance verb morphology actually ran — that half is the
stronger one. *Regular and irregular interleaved within one tense* has no direct
support that could be found; it is a plausible extension, not a finding. Build
the first with confidence and the second as a hypothesis.

Four variants:

| Variant | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Fill the table** | verb + person → enter the form |
| **Backwards** | form given → which verb, which person? |
| **Transform** | "He goes." → perfect, subjunctive, negated |
| **Compare** | *good · better · best* — irregulars first |

Sheets are assembled from **your** errors: which forms went wrong in
conversations, dictations and cloze sentences.

> **Elevated on 2026-08-08:** since form mastery is its own level signal
> ([03](03-level-model.md)), these tables are no longer a side exercise but the
> training for a measured quantity.

## Ü4 · Without a screen and without paper

The overlooked case: out and about, hands and eyes occupied.

- **Audiobook with voice commands** — the main scenario, see
  [05](05-input-reading-listening.md).
- **Audio flashcards**: question read out, pause, answer. Fully operable blind
  via "knew it" / "did not" by voice or headphone button.
- **Self-talk with a task**: "describe what you can see, for ten minutes." No
  feedback, but the only exercise that trains *speaking fluency* under real time
  pressure. Optionally record and review later ([06](06-production.md)).

## Ü5 · With other people

Not a software problem, but the app can prepare rather than broker:

- **Conversation cards** for a real tandem partner: 10 questions at your level,
  using the vocabulary you are currently learning.
- **A preparation sheet** before a planned conversation (doctor, government
  office, job interview) — the 30 words and 10 phrases that will come up.
- **Debrief**: "what could you not say?" → type three sentences → cards. The
  highest-value card source there is, because the need is demonstrated.

---

## What goes into a spec

- The **print contract**: A4, black and white, no colour dependency, answer key
  on a separable page. Sounds banal; it is the difference between usable and not.
- The **feedback format**: how an offline result becomes a card event, and what
  happens if a sheet is never returned (answer: nothing — it must create **no**
  backlog, or the backlog trap from [04](04-flashcards-srs.md) applies).
- Whether offline results count **equally** in [03](03-level-model.md). **[D]**
  Proposal: yes, but marked as a separate source — self-correction is more
  generous than a machine, and that should be visible.
