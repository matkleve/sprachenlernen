## Decisions

- **Study in German, code and specs in English.** Chose the split over
  all-German because the study is read and argued with while the code is read
  and executed. Rules out German identifiers; `GLOSSARY.md` therefore carries
  both columns. Open for reversal — question 9 in the roadmap.
- **FSRS over SM-2**, and the deciding reason is *not* the accuracy benchmark.
  It is that stability / difficulty / retrievability are quantities a human can
  be shown, and "ease factor 2.35" is not. The better-scheduling algorithm
  happening to also be the explainable one is what makes the transparency
  feature buildable at all.
- **Vocabulary size is the load-bearing measurement.** Chose it over
  task-success scores because it is the only quantity that bridges review data
  and the CEFR level, via frequency rank. Rules out a level model that could be
  built before frequency lists and lemmatisation exist — hence stage 0.
- **Overall level = second-lowest skill**, not the average. The average hides a
  gap, and the gap is the actionable part.
- **No leagues, hearts or XP.** Chose the consistent position over a partial
  one. This is the study's weakest-evidenced decision and it is labelled as such
  in `08-motivation.md` — the evidence says reliably what harms, much less
  reliably what works instead.
- **Grundriss's worked example stays for now.** Chose keeping `item-picker`,
  UC-001…003 and the primitives over deleting them, because no real feature
  demonstrates those patterns yet. Our use cases therefore start at UC-004.
  Delete when the first real feature lands (README step 5).

## Later the same day — method cards

User proposed: a daily choice of practice methods, each shown as a card with its
intensity and what it trains; thumbs up/down after a session; an algorithm
learns from that but can still say "this is important once a week anyway".

Researched it before writing, and the finding changed the design. Kornell &
Bjork: learners rated blocked practice as more effective than interleaved
**after taking a test they had just done better on with interleaving**. A
follow-up paper is literally titled *On the Difficulty of Mending Metacognitive
Illusions*. So the thumbs signal is systematically biased **against** the
methods that work.

### Decisions

- **Preference and effect are two separate ledgers, never combined into one
  number.** Preference (thumbs) governs form — length, timing, share, framing.
  Measured effect governs selection. Chose this over a single "method score"
  because once they are merged the distinction is unrecoverable and nothing
  surfaces the loss. Written into `GLOSSARY.md` as a normative sentence, not
  only into the study.
- **The user's "important once a week anyway" became the Floor**, and it is what
  keeps the whole mechanism from degenerating. Two properties make it a rule
  rather than nagging: it states its reason in terms of the learner's own data,
  and it negotiates over *length*, never over existence. Plus a hard cap of one
  floor prompt per day across all methods.
- **Three options in the daily menu, not a catalogue.** Choice supports autonomy,
  but the same literature says weak self-regulators are overwhelmed by too much
  of it — which is exactly beginners. Rules out a "pick any exercise" screen.
- **Thumbs down asks one follow-up question.** "Too long", "too hard" and
  "boring" have three different fixes and only one of them is about the method.
- **Effect estimation is labelled as statistically weak and deferred (F94/F95).**
  Tiny n, heavy confounding, slow feedback. Population priors first, deliberate
  exploration to get any causal footing at all, uncertainty always displayed.
  Chose saying so over shipping a confident-looking number.

### Decisions

- **HVPT gets its own chapter and becomes thesis 7.** Meta-analysis over 79
  studies: g ≈ 0.92 pre/post, g ≈ 0.67 vs. control, gains generalise to unheard
  words *and unheard speakers*, and perceptual training alone transfers to
  production. Best evidence-to-effort ratio in the whole study: no LLM, no ASR,
  no scoring of user audio. Rules out treating pronunciation as a production
  problem — chapter 06's pronunciation section is now explicitly the leftovers.
- **The talker pool size is an invariant, not a production parameter.** High
  variability *is* the mechanism. Training with too few speakers looks identical
  and does nothing, which makes it the most dangerous place to economise. Said
  so in the spec requirements rather than in a note.
- **Skill profile is configurable, and it lands in stage 2, not later.** A deaf
  user under the current model gets a permanently depressed overall level. That
  is a calculation error, not a display issue, and retrofitting it means bending
  every user's history.
- **No sleep feature**, despite a real effect existing. It needs an EEG headband
  and failed to replicate in adolescents. Kept the banal part (evening session
  as an option, no efficacy claim) and said why the rest is out.

### Corrections to earlier chapters

- **Chapter 05 overclaimed.** LingQ and Migaku have done coverage-based reading
  against a known-word inventory for years. Our difference is narrower than
  written — a pre-open coverage figure, the feedback loop to recently learned
  cards, and feeding a level model. Corrected in place with a pointer to 15/K1;
  "nobody else does this" is struck.
- The real differentiator is the **closed loop** (SRS → level → input → SRS),
  not any single ring. Which also names the real risk: every ring is
  individually worse than the specialist tool. That risk was nowhere in the
  study before.
- W4 turned up post-hoc support for chapter 08: the *ought-to* L2 self measurably
  raises anxiety while the *ideal* L2 self lowers it. A lapsing streak is an
  ought-to mechanic. Two independent literatures now point the same way.

### Went wrong

- Chapter 05 shipped a "nobody else does this" claim that a competitor pass
  would have caught immediately. The cause is sequencing: product reasoning was
  written before looking at what exists. The landscape chapter should have come
  before chapter 05, not after chapter 12. Noted rather than promoted — it is a
  habit for the next study, not a trap in this codebase.

## Fourth pass — review, a decision, and 16 more use cases

### Correction from the user

- User: *"Methoden können versteckt sein"* → answers open question 11. A method
  **may** be hidden. This does not overturn A15: that anti-pattern forbids the
  **algorithm** from dropping a method, not the **person** from deciding. The
  distinction is now written into both places, plus the rules that keep it a
  decision rather than a frustrated tap (settings only, one costed explanation
  from their own data, stays visibly hidden, restorable) — and the consequence
  is drawn rather than hidden: hiding every method feeding a skill makes that
  skill "not measured", never a low number.

### Went wrong — found by the review pass, not by a gate

- **One rule, three owners.** "Not measured" was described independently in
  chapters 03, 12 and 14, each slightly differently, and UC-004 had a fourth
  version. It had already drifted: 14 said a de-selected skill is *not* "not
  measured", 12 said hiding methods *makes* it "not measured", and nothing said
  whether either counts toward the overall level. Now a four-state table owned
  by chapter 03 alone (measured · uncertain · not measured · not in profile),
  everything else links to it, and it is F110 in stage 2.
- **The overall-level formula was wrong for small profiles.** "Second-lowest of
  the four" was written before chapter 14 made profiles with fewer than four
  skills possible. With two skills, second-lowest *is* the highest — the most
  optimistic answer, in the one case built for people the model already
  disadvantages. Now: second-lowest from three, minimum at two, undefined at
  one.
- Both are the same failure in different clothes: a rule written in the chapter
  that needed it rather than in the chapter that owns it. `check:specs` cannot
  catch this — it verifies links resolve, not that a normative sentence has one
  home. Worth watching for whether it recurs before promoting it.
- Four V1 features (F83, F85, F103, F107) were in no roadmap stage at all. They
  are cross-cutting, so they were never "due" — now a Querschnitt section.
