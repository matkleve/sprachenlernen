## The iPhone question

Asked whether iOS could do the grammar check for us. It cannot, and the reason
matters enough to record: **no web API exposes spellcheck results to
JavaScript**, and iOS checks against the device's *installed keyboard
languages*, not the page language. A learner with a German keyboard would get
their Spanish silently rewritten, and our checker would then flag errors the
phone introduced. `autocorrect="off"` and `spellCheck={false}` on the sentence
field follow from that.

## What the checker may say

Four rules, all provable from the shipped lemma table: `spelling`, `agreement`,
`person`, `missing-target`. Word order, word choice, idiom and style are out of
reach and stay silent. The load-bearing rule is that a finding needs the clash
to hold under **every** reading the table allows — one innocent reading clears
the token. `lib/sentence-check/corpus.test.ts` guards the quiet half: twenty
correct sentences that must produce no findings.

Evidence: Lyster & Ranta via [`STUDY-006`](../study/STUDY-006-production.md) —
prompts beat recasts, and `reveal-answer` was a recast. The same chapter is why
green cannot claim correctness.

## Two data facts that cost a debug round each

- **Nouns carry gender on the lemma, not the cell.** `forms.casa` is
  `[["casa","noun","sg"]]`; the `f` lives in `lemmas.casa`. Reading gender off
  the cell made every noun genderless, and a genderless noun agrees with
  everything — the whole agreement rule was silently dead. Adjectives are the
  opposite: their cell has the gender, and their lemma entry describes the noun
  sense of the same string (`blanco` → `m`), which fights every feminine form.
- **The reverse index needs a tie-break.** `el` at `f.sg` is `la`, but a
  treebank also annotates the stray token `a` as a form of `el`; at `m.pl` both
  `los` and an imported `els` sit at rank 0. Ranking by how central the reading
  is to each form fixes the first; the second has no principled winner in this
  data, so no suggestion is offered at all.

## Wood texture lab — interrupted ridges (Trivial, `lib/` only)

`/dev/wood-textures`'s canvas grain (`lib/wood-grain-ridges.ts`) looked wrong
in a way that took three rounds of owner feedback to pin down. All three
rounds, in the owner's words: first "brighter or less bright stripes
vertically" (an `feTurbulence` frequency pairing backwards — fixed by going
CSS-only, then abandoned for canvas); then "the hills and valleys dont pull
through from left to right they are interrupted" — this session's fix.

**What "interrupted" means, precisely** (the owner asked me to restate it,
which is itself worth recording): a ridge that should read as one continuous
wavy line, smoothly bending up and down as it crosses the swatch, instead
visibly folds or breaks — for a short stretch the line degenerates into a
tight tangle of small zigzags, then resumes smoothly. Not a style
preference; the traced curve is mathematically no longer single-valued in
that stretch.

**Two distinct causes, both about the warp changing too fast relative to the
grid it bends:**

1. The warp's vertical noise input was `y / height` — across one ridge's own
   height, that moved the noise field by enough that the same ridge sampled
   a different warp near its own top vs its own bottom. Fixed by sampling in
   ridge units (`y / ridgeStep`) at a small fixed correlation constant.
2. The warp's horizontal rate of change could exceed one ridge-step within
   one warp wavelength — `warpAmount × warpFrequency` was highest on exactly
   the "Raw planks" preset the owner was looking at (`0.9 × 3.2`). Added
   `stableWarpAmount()`, which caps the amplitude per render so it can never
   outrun the card's actual pixel width — narrow/zoomed renders are where
   the instability is most visible, since a coarse render just blurs it away
   instead of fixing it.

Both are now regression-tested on the warp function directly
(`lib/wood-grain-ridges.test.ts`), not by inspecting rendered pixels — a
pixel-diff test would have missed this the same way a screenshot review
already had. Full reasoning: [STUDY-030](../study/STUDY-030-procedural-wood-grain.md)
§ interrupted ridges.

**Change-completeness:** `docs/specs/page/wood-texture-lab.md` still named
`lib/wood-grain.ts` from before this file was renamed to
`lib/wood-grain-ridges.ts` (to avoid colliding with the unrelated
`wood-grain-lab` feature that landed on `main` in parallel) — fixed in the
same change.

### Open

- The stability cap uses an empirical slope bound (`WARP_FIELD_MAX_SLOPE =
  2.5`) for two-octave value noise; if a future preset adds more octaves or
  changes the noise function, that constant needs re-deriving, not just
  reusing.
- Not re-verified against the actual `design/progression/reference-board.png`

## Wood texture lab — Wilkie distortion field (follow-up, same session)

Owner supplied Hafidi & Wilkie (CGF 2025) and jsabbott Blender tutorial URLs
and asked why research was not done first. Read both (ResearchGate blocked;
used DOI abstract, cgg.mff.cuni.cz, arXiv 2302.01820, Cornell LDHM16 paper,
oldetinkererstudio.com mirror for jsabbott).

**What the literature actually says:** growth rings are sampled at a
**distorted** position f(p). Radial distortion mr(p) bends ring shapes;
tangential mt(p) adds figure; Hafidi adds **influence points** (repulsive
ring displacement). Not `sin(y + f(x))` warped stripes.

**Pivot:** `ringAgeAt(x,y) = base_y + mr(x,y) + mt(x,y) + influence(x,y)`
with multiband fbm (4 bands). Ridges = contours of sin(ringAge). Documented in
STUDY-030 § owner-requested sources; sources added to STUDY-sources.md.
